323 Stores, 306K Customers

Lenovo Baiying private domain: turning scattered customers into an operable asset

The hard part of private-domain operations is not acquisition. It is getting three hundred stores to move the same way. My first move was not targets — it was a definition of the flow: how a merchant executes, how customer quality is scored, how content formats are ranked, how feedback is collected. Four steps, each with its own standard.

Then: implementation guides, store replacement, sharing of what works, regional coordination, live dashboards. Stores that hit the mark are put on screen in the weekly meeting; the ones that don't go on a replacement list. The rules come first, the data only checks them.

The result: 171K customers to 306K (+78.9%), 181 stores to 323 (+78.5%), and 173K new customers — 129% of target, the highest of all nine KPIs.

Only one metric missed: repeat orders, at 71%, a gap of 5,120. The causes are specific — stores without SaaS leave no trackable data, and high-frequency services like screen protectors were never linked to low-frequency ones like repair. That is the next thing to take apart.
